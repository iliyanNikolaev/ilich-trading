STRATEGY MODEL · LESSON 11POWER OF THREE

AMD and Premium / Discount

A practical framework that views the market through three phases—Accumulation, Manipulation and Distribution—and uses a price range to distinguish relatively expensive and cheaper areas where traders may look for opportunities.

AccumulationManipulationDistributionPremium / Discount

This is an educational model, not an exact reconstruction of an internal automated algorithm. The examples are illustrative and are not live trading signals.

01

The Core Idea

The market sometimes moves from a range to a false breakout and then into a stronger directional move.

A · ACCUMULATION

Accumulation

Price moves within a relatively narrow range. Buyers and sellers are temporarily balanced.

M · MANIPULATION

Manipulation / sweep

Price briefly moves above the range high or below the range low, potentially triggering stops and accessing liquidity, but may then return into the range.

D · DISTRIBUTION

Distribution

After a false breakout, a stronger move in the opposite direction may begin. The move should be confirmed by market structure.

AMD is not a fixed intraday sequence

Not every range produces a clear manipulation and distribution phase. Sometimes price makes a genuine breakout and continues in the same direction. Do not assume every move outside a range is false.

02

What are Premium and Discount?

We divide a selected price range into an upper half and a lower half.

Choose a clear swing range—from a significant low to a significant high. Find its midpoint (Equilibrium, 50%). Below the midpoint is Discount—the lower half of the range. Above it is Premium—the upper half.

PREMIUMAbove 50% · relatively more expensive half
50% · EQUILIBRIUM
DISCOUNTBelow 50% · relatively cheaper half
Example range
Low2 000
High2 100
Midpoint (50%)2 050

Below 2,050, price is in the Discount area of this range; above 2,050, it is in the Premium area.

For a BUY

We look for a potential entry in Discount and plan an exit towards a previous high, the upper part of the range or another area of potential liquidity in Premium.

For a SELL

We look for a potential entry in Premium and plan an exit towards a previous low, the lower part of the range or another area of potential liquidity in Discount.

Cheaper does not automatically mean BUY

Discount is not a buy signal, and Premium is not a sell signal. The higher-timeframe trend, liquidity and confirmation are essential context.

03

Bullish scenario – looking for a BUY

The idea is to see a sweep below the range followed by a confirmed recovery.

  1. Define the range.For example, price trades between 2,000 and 2,100. The midpoint is 2,050.
  2. Watch for a sweep below the low.Price falls to 1,992, briefly breaks below 2,000 and then moves back above 2,000.
  3. Wait for bullish confirmation.Price makes a strong move upward and breaks the latest significant swing high (bullish BOS / market structure shift).
  4. Look for a return into Discount.During a pullback into the lower half of the range or a defined area of interest, watch whether buyers defend the price.
  5. Plan a BUY.Consider an entry only after a reaction and confirmation; place the stop beyond the structural level that invalidates the idea.
BUY logic

Range → brief break below the low → move back above the low → bullish BOS → pullback into Discount / area of interest → BUY only after confirmation.

04

Bearish scenario – looking for a SELL

The opposite scenario: a sweep above the range followed by a confirmed move downward.

  1. Define the range.For example, price trades between 2,000 and 2,100. The midpoint is 2,050.
  2. Watch for a sweep above the high.Price rises to 2,108, moves above 2,100 and then returns below 2,100.
  3. Wait for bearish confirmation.Price makes a strong move downward and breaks the latest significant swing low.
  4. Look for a return into Premium.During a pullback into the upper half of the range or an area of interest, watch whether sellers defend the price.
  5. Plan a SELL.Consider an entry only after a bearish reaction; place the stop beyond the structural level that invalidates the idea.
SELL logic

Range → brief break above the high → move back below the high → bearish BOS → pullback into Premium / area of interest → SELL only after confirmation.

05

What does “liquidity” mean in this model?

Areas around which many market participants may have placed stop-loss or pending orders.

Above previous highs

There may be stop-loss orders from sellers and buy-stop orders above previous highs. A brief move above a high is sometimes called a buy-side liquidity sweep.

Below previous lows

There may be stop-loss orders from buyers and sell-stop orders below previous lows. A brief move below a low is sometimes called a sell-side liquidity sweep.

A chart does not show us every order in the market. “Liquidity” is therefore a working interpretation of likely areas, not proof that we know exactly where all stops are located. In AMD, “Manipulation” is the name of a model phase; it does not by itself prove intentional or illegal market manipulation.

06

What can SMT add?

An additional comparison with another related market.

If price sweeps above a high but a related instrument does not confirm the new move, this may provide an additional clue of weakness. The same applies to a sweep below a low. SMT is only an additional clue—it does not replace market structure and is not required for every setup.

07

When should we avoid this model?

  • There is no clear range from which to measure Premium / Discount.
  • Price breaks out of the range and holds outside it—it may be a genuine breakout, not a manipulation phase.
  • Price does not return into the range after the sweep.
  • There is no BOS or other clear change in short-term structure.
  • There is not enough room to the target, or the risk is too large.
Do not look for manipulation in every move

The model is useful only when there is a clear sequence and predefined conditions. Trying to explain every candle as a “stop hunt” can lead to rushed decisions.

Quick check

The range is 2,000–2,100. Where is the midpoint, and which part is Premium?

Answer: The midpoint is 2,050. Above 2,050 is Premium; below 2,050 is Discount.
