Two-Candle Patterns

Two-candle patterns consist of two consecutive candles. They show how control may shift between buyers and sellers. The sequence, candle bodies, wicks and preceding context all matter.

EngulfingTweezerInside / Outside
01

Engulfing

The second candle’s body covers the body of the first candle.

BULLISH ENGULFING

Bullish Engulfing

  • The first candle is bearish.
  • The second candle is bullish, and its body covers the first candle’s body.
  • After a decline, it may indicate strengthening buying pressure.
BEARISH ENGULFING

Bearish Engulfing

  • The first candle is bullish.
  • The second candle is bearish, and its body covers the first candle’s body.
  • After a rise, it may indicate strengthening selling pressure.
02

Tweezer Top and Tweezer Bottom

Two candles with highs or lows at similar price levels.

Tweezer Top

After a rise, two candles reach similar highs. This may suggest rejection of the area.

Tweezer Bottom

After a decline, two candles reach similar lows. This may suggest buyers are defending the area.

03

Inside Bar and Outside Bar

Here we compare the full range of both candles, not just their bodies.

Inside Bar

The second candle has a lower High and a higher Low than the first candle. This shows a contraction in price movement and a temporary pause.

It can be used to watch for a breakout, but the direction is not known in advance.

Outside Bar

The second candle has a High above the first candle’s High and a Low below its Low. The range expands and volatility increases.

The closing direction and market context help with interpretation.

04

Dark Cloud Cover and Piercing Line

Patterns that may indicate weakening of the preceding move.

Dark Cloud Cover

  • The first candle is strongly bullish.
  • The second candle is bearish and closes below the midpoint of the first candle’s body.
  • After a rise, it may signal that sellers are returning.

Piercing Line

  • The first candle is strongly bearish.
  • The second candle is bullish and closes above the midpoint of the first candle’s body.
  • After a decline, it may signal that buyers are returning.

Classic descriptions often include a price gap at the open. In markets that trade almost continuously, such as FX CFDs, these gaps may be absent. Focus on the pattern’s core shape and close rather than relying on a gap alone.

05

How to apply a two-candle pattern?

  1. Check the preceding move.A reversal pattern is more meaningful after a clear preceding trend.
  2. Check where it appears.Support, resistance or another important area adds context.
  3. Wait for the second candle to close.The candle’s shape can change while it is still forming.
  4. Look for confirmation.Do not enter simply because you recognise the name of a pattern.
The pattern is not a guarantee

No two-candle pattern is automatically “more reliable” in every situation. Results depend on the instrument, timeframe, context and entry and exit rules.
