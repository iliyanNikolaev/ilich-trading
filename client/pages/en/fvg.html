Fair Value Gap (FVG)

An FVG is a price area where a strong move leaves little or no overlap between the wicks of the first and third candles in a three-candle sequence. Traders watch it as a potential area for a price return.

3 candlesDisplacementPotential zone
01

What does an FVG show?

A fast move in one direction with relatively little overlap around it.

Imagine three consecutive candles. The middle candle makes a strong move. If the ranges of the first and third candles do not overlap in a certain area, a zone forms that is often called a Fair Value Gap or imbalance.

Important clarification

An FVG is not a literal gap in the exchange order book and does not mean price must return to “fill” it. It is a way of describing a price inefficiency within a specific candlestick pattern.

02

How to identify it?

The first and third candles define the boundaries of the zone.

BULLISH FVG

Bullish FVG

  • The middle candle makes a strong move upward.
  • The Low of the third candle is above the High of the first.
  • The area between the first candle’s High and the third candle’s Low is the bullish FVG.
Example

Candle 1 High = 2,010. Candle 3 Low = 2,016. The FVG is approximately 2,010–2,016.

BEARISH FVG

Bearish FVG

  • The middle candle makes a strong move downward.
  • The High of the third candle is below the Low of the first.
  • The area between the third candle’s High and the first candle’s Low is the bearish FVG.
Example

Candle 1 Low = 2,010. Candle 3 High = 2,004. The FVG is approximately 2,004–2,010.

03

How can it be used?

An FVG is an area to watch, not a standalone signal.

For a BUY idea

  1. The higher-timeframe context is bullish.
  2. A strong move upward creates a bullish FVG.
  3. Price returns to the zone.
  4. Look for a reaction and confirmation that the move may continue upward.

For a SELL idea

  1. The higher-timeframe context is bearish.
  2. A strong move downward creates a bearish FVG.
  3. Price moves back up into the zone.
  4. Look for a reaction and confirmation that the move may continue downward.
Do not treat every return as an entry

Sometimes price moves straight through an FVG without reacting; sometimes it never returns. The zone is more meaningful when it aligns with the trend, a key level or clear market structure.

04

What should you check before using it?

  • On which timeframe did the FVG form?
  • Is there a clear impulse rather than just three random candles?
  • Does its direction align with the higher-timeframe market structure?
  • Is there enough room to the next support or resistance area?
  • Where is the idea invalidated, and what is the monetary risk?
Quick check

The first candle’s High is 2,100 and the third candle’s Low is 2,108. What type of FVG may form?

Answer: A bullish FVG between 2,100 and 2,108, if the three-candle structure matches the described pattern.
