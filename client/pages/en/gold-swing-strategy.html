A Pullback Is Not Necessarily a Reversal

During a bearish H4 trend, H1 may temporarily form higher highs and higher lows while price rises in a pullback. During a bullish H4 trend, H1 may temporarily form lower highs and lower lows while price falls in a pullback.

03

Market Structure: Four Key Concepts

First, learn to identify swing highs and swing lows.

Bullish structure

HH – Higher HighThe new high is higher than the previous high.
HL – Higher LowThe new low is higher than the previous low.

A sequence of HHs and HLs shows that buyers remain in control.

Bearish structure

LH – Lower HighThe new high is lower than the previous high.
LL – Lower LowThe new low is lower than the previous low.

A sequence of LHs and LLs shows that sellers remain in control.

HL example
Previous low2 050
New low2 075

2,075 is higher than 2,050 – this is a Higher Low.

LH example
Previous high2 130
New high2 110

2,110 is lower than 2,130 – this is a Lower High.

A local high or low should be clearly formed: price moves away from it and a reaction is visible. Do not mark every small fluctuation as a new swing. Use wicks to identify extreme prices, but watch candle closes when confirming a breakout.

04

What Are BOS and a Retest?

These two concepts connect local market structure with a potential entry.

BEARISH BOS

Bearish Break of Structure

After a Lower High, price closes below the latest relevant local low. In this model, this confirms that sellers are taking control again.

Example

Local low 3,568 → H1 closes at 3,564. The candle has closed below the level.

BULLISH BOS

Bullish Break of Structure

After a Higher Low, price closes above the latest relevant local high. In this model, this confirms that buyers are taking control again.

Example

Local high 3,568 → H1 closes at 3,574. The candle has closed above the level.

A Wick Alone Is Not Enough for Our Confirmation

If price wicks below a local low but the candle closes back above it, we do not record this as a confirmed BOS under this model’s strict rules. The opposite applies to BUY setups.

BOS vs. MSS / CHoCH

In this guide, BOS means a break in the direction of the current structure—for example, a break of a low in a bearish trend or a high in a bullish trend. MSS / CHoCH usually describes the first break against the previous direction and may warn of a possible change. Traders use these terms differently, so always define your own rules.

Retest for a SELL

After a close below the local low, price moves back up towards the broken level from underneath. We watch whether it acts as resistance and whether a bearish reaction appears.

Retest for a BUY

After a close above the local high, price pulls back towards the broken level from above. We watch whether it acts as support and whether a bullish reaction appears.

Do Not Confuse Two Different Tests

First, price may test the larger H4 zone (for example, former support that has turned into resistance). Then, after an H1 BOS, there may be a separate retest of the local level that has just been broken. These are different levels and different stages. A regular pullback into a zone does not always require a separate zone retest followed by a second retest—the specific model you have chosen and tested is what matters.

05

Practical BUY Setup – Uptrend

We wait for the pullback to finish and look for confirmation of continuation to the upside.

  1. D1/H4 show an uptrend.There is a sequence of higher highs (HH) and higher lows (HL).
  2. Price pulls back downward.Price returns to a previously marked support or demand area.
  3. H1 forms a Higher Low.Example: previous low 3,520; the pullback stops at 3,535 – the new low is higher.
  4. H1 makes a bullish BOS.The local high is 3,560; H1 closes above it, for example at 3,566.
  5. Wait for the retest.Price returns to 3,560–3,566 from above and fails to stay below the level.
  6. A bullish reaction appears.After confirmation, we consider a potential BUY. We do not buy simply because price has touched support.
Illustrative BUY example

Support → low at 3,535 (HL) → break above 3,560 (BOS) → pullback to 3,560 from above → bullish reaction → potential BUY.

06

Practical SELL Setup – Downtrend

We wait for the upward pullback to finish and look for confirmation of continuation to the downside.

  1. D1/H4 show a downtrend.There is a sequence of lower highs (LH) and lower lows (LL).
  2. Price moves upward in a pullback.Price returns to a previously marked resistance or supply area.
  3. H1 forms a Lower High.Example: previous high 3,585; after a decline, price rises only to 3,578.
  4. H1 makes a bearish BOS.The local low is 3,568; H1 closes below it, for example at 3,564.
  5. Wait for the retest.Price returns to 3,568–3,570 from underneath but fails to hold above the level.
  6. A bearish reaction appears.After confirmation, we consider a potential SELL. We do not sell simply because price has touched resistance.
Illustrative SELL example

Resistance → high at 3,578 (LH) → break below 3,568 (BOS) → return to 3,568 from underneath → bearish reaction → potential SELL.

07

When Do We Skip a Trade?

A strategy should also tell us when not to trade.

Insufficient confirmation

  • Price only touches the zone.
  • There is an LH but no bearish BOS (or an HL but no bullish BOS).
  • There is a BOS, but no retest according to the model.
  • The candle has not closed yet.

Poor conditions

  • The entry is too close to the next significant zone.
  • The Stop Loss is too large for the account.
  • The minimum lot would result in an unacceptable loss.
  • An important event is approaching and there is no volatility plan.
08

Stop Loss, Take Profit and Risk

First identify the structural invalidation level, then decide whether the position size and risk are acceptable.

For a BUY

The stop is usually below the Higher Low or another level that invalidates the bullish scenario. The first logical target may be the next resistance or a previous high.

For a SELL

The stop is usually above the Lower High or another level that invalidates the bearish scenario. The first logical target may be the next support or a previous low.

Do Not Tighten the Stop Just to Fit the Trade

Calculate the potential loss at the minimum lot using the actual contract specifications. If the risk is too large, skip the trade or practise it on demo. A wider stop requires a smaller position size, not a larger risk.

Key Takeaways

H4 tells us where and in what context to look. H1 shows whether a setup is forming. In an uptrend, we look for HL → bullish BOS → retest → BUY. In a downtrend, we look for LH → bearish BOS → retest → SELL. If the conditions are missing or the risk is too high, there is no trade.

Record every example, including missed and invalid setups. Evaluate the strategy after a series of observations, not after a single trade.
