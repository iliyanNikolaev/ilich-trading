TRADING BASICS

What Is Trading?

A practical introduction for complete beginners. Learn how prices move, what BUY and SELL mean, how to open a position, and why risk management matters more than searching for a “perfect signal.”

01

What does a trader actually do?

A trader tries to make decisions based on the market’s likely price movement.

BUY / LONG

You expect the price to rise

You open a BUY position when you expect the price to rise. The position gains value as the price moves up and loses value if the market moves against it.

ExampleGold 2,000 → 2,020
SELL / SHORT

You expect the price to fall

You open a SELL position when you expect the price to fall. With a CFD, you can speculate on a falling market without owning physical gold.

ExampleGold 2,000 → 1,980
Important distinction

BUY and SELL describe the direction of your position; they do not guarantee that your forecast will be correct. Any trade can result in a loss.

02

What is XAU/USD?

The instrument used throughout the examples.

XAU

Gold

The code XAU represents one troy ounce of gold.

USD

US dollar

The currency in which gold is quoted.

PRICE

Price per ounce

If XAU/USD is 2,000, it means approximately USD 2,000 per troy ounce.

Gold is influenced by supply and demand, interest-rate expectations, the US dollar, bond yields, inflation, central banks and geopolitical events. No single factor determines the price on its own.

03

What determines the outcome of a trade?

Profit and loss depend on the price movement and the size of your position.

Position size (lots)

A lot represents the size of your position. For the same price movement, a larger position will generally produce a larger profit or loss.

Spread

The difference between the price at which you can buy (Ask) and the price at which you can sell (Bid). It is one of the costs of trading.

Stop Loss (SL)

An order placed in advance to limit a loss if the price moves against you. During sharp moves, the order may be executed at a different price.

Take Profit (TP)

A target set in advance at which the position closes in profit if the market reaches that level.

Illustrative example

You open a BUY at 2,000 and close it at 2,010. The price has risen by 10 dollars. The actual result depends on lot size, contract specifications, spread, commissions and your account currency.

04

What is a CFD, and what do you not own?

Before opening a live account, understand the product you are trading.

CFD = Contract for Difference

With a CFD, you speculate on changes in an asset’s price. You generally do not own physical gold or the underlying share. Your profit or loss is based on the difference between the opening and closing prices and the contract size.

A CFD may use leverage. This means margin (collateral) is set aside to open a position, while profit and loss are calculated on the full notional position. Leverage does not make a trade safer.

05

How does price move?

A chart records the interaction between buyers and sellers.

DEMAND

Buyers

When buyers are more aggressive and accept higher prices, the market may rise.

SUPPLY

Sellers

When sellers are more aggressive and accept lower prices, the market may fall.

BALANCE

Consolidation

When neither side has a clear advantage, price may move sideways within a range.

06

How to learn step by step?

You do not need to learn every indicator at once.

Before your first live trade

Practise on a demo account, record your examples, and make sure you understand lots, margin, spread and stop-loss orders. Never trade money you need for everyday expenses.

Check your understanding

Question: You open a SELL position on XAU/USD. Which price movement would make the position profitable before costs?

Answer: When the price falls. If the price rises, the position is at a loss.
