Risk Management

Before thinking about how much you can make, decide how much you can lose. Risk management does not make a strategy profitable, but it helps prevent one bad trade from damaging your account severely.

01

How much do you risk per trade?

Risk is the amount you lose if the stop is executed near the planned level.

Example using a €100 practice account
1% risk1 €
2% risk2 €
3% risk3 €
5% risk5 €

These are arithmetic examples, not a recommendation for a specific risk percentage. Even a small percentage does not eliminate the risk of loss.

02

What is a Stop Loss?

The level at which the position is closed if the trade idea is no longer valid.

For a BUY, the stop is often placed below the structural low that invalidates the bullish scenario. For a SELL, it is often placed above the structural high that invalidates the bearish scenario. The chart determines the level; monetary risk determines position size.

BUY position

If the idea is that support and the latest Higher Low will hold, plan the stop below the level where that idea is no longer valid.

SELL position

If the idea is that resistance and the latest Lower High will hold, plan the stop above the level where the bearish scenario is no longer valid.

A stop does not guarantee the exact price

During news, price gaps, low liquidity or fast moves, execution may be worse than the requested price (slippage). Check your broker’s terms.

03

What is position size?

It determines how much money you gain or lose for a given price movement.

A wider Stop Loss does not necessarily mean a larger monetary loss. If the stop distance increases, position size should be reduced to keep the risk amount unchanged. If the minimum lot is too large for the planned risk, the trade may not be suitable for that account.

General calculation logic

Approximate formula: position size = allowed loss ÷ (stop distance × monetary value of the move per 1 lot).

Then check the result against the minimum lot, Contract Size, Tick Size, Tick Value and account currency. The exact value depends on the specific contract and broker specifications.

04

Leverage and Margin

Leverage changes the collateral requirement; it does not make risk free.

Leverage

It allows you to control a position with a notional value greater than the capital you set aside as collateral.

Margin

The amount the broker reserves to maintain an open position. Margin is not your maximum possible loss.

A simple margin example

For a position with a notional value of €1,000 and 1:10 leverage, the approximate initial margin is €100. If the position moves against you, the loss is calculated on the notional position, not only on the margin.

Important

Leverage increases exposure and can lead to rapid losses, a margin call or forced position closure. Do not choose leverage based on how large a position it lets you open.

05

Risk / Reward

Compare potential profit with potential loss.

1 : 1

Equal potential

The planned profit equals the planned loss.

1 : 2

Twice the potential

The planned profit is twice the planned loss.

1 : 3

Three times the potential

The planned profit is three times the planned loss.

Risk/reward does not tell you how often you will win. A strategy with a high reward relative to its stop can still lose frequently. Evaluation requires a large sample of recorded trades, win rate, average win, average loss and trading costs.

06

Costs you should not forget

Spread and commission

The difference between Bid and Ask, along with any commission, reduces the net result.

Swap / overnight

Holding a CFD position overnight may incur financing or swap charges. Check the instrument and broker terms.

Slippage

The difference between the requested and actual execution price, especially during fast market moves.

Currency conversion

If the instrument is quoted in USD while your account is in EUR, the result may be converted into your account currency.

07

Rules before every trade

  • I know why I am entering and what confirms the idea.
  • I know where the idea becomes invalid.
  • I know how many euros I will lose if the stop is hit.
  • I have checked the minimum lot and the monetary value of the price movement.
  • I have checked the spread, commissions and swap charges.
  • I do not need to move the stop farther away to avoid taking a loss.
  • I can monitor the position, or it is protected by orders placed in advance.
  • I am prepared to skip the trade if the risk is too large.
With a small account and a minimum lot

If the technically correct stop would mean an unacceptably large loss, do not make the stop artificially tighter. Skip the trade or practise the setup on a demo account until you can apply it with an appropriate position size.
